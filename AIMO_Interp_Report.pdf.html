Layerwise Representation Stability and Early-Trace Spectral Entropy for White-Box Detection of Spurious Mathematical Reasoning

Competition: NeurIPS 2026 Official Competition — AIMO Interpretability Challenge ($12,500 USD Prize Pool)
Eligible Tracks: Main Track ($5,000), Small Models Track ($3,500), White-Box Award (+$2,000), Writeup Track ($4,000)
Submission Artifacts: aimo_main_track.zip (solution.py), aimo_small_track.zip (solution.py + small.txt)

Abstract

Large reasoning models (LRMs) trained with reinforcement learning on mathematical chains of thought frequently achieve high accuracy on canonical benchmark phrasings while failing catastrophically under semantically preserving re-framings—a phenomenon known as spurious or brittle mathematical reasoning. Crucially, the AIMO Interpretability Challenge evaluates whether a detector can predict a target model's robustness across unseen surface variations given only a single problem phrasing and white-box access to the model weights. We introduce LRS-RTE (Layerwise Representation Stability and Reasoning Trace Entropy), a training-free white-box mechanistic probe that operates in two stages: (1) constructing a deterministic, mathematically isomorphic counterfactual prompt via bound-variable transposition ($x \leftrightarrow y, n \leftrightarrow k, a \leftrightarrow b$) and measuring the residual-stream cosine stability $\mathcal{S}{\cos}$ and $\ell_2$-norm preservation across the upper half ($50\%, 75\%, 90\%, 100\%$ depth) of the transformer at the reasoning launchpad token; and (2) rolling out a short $K$-step greedy reasoning prefix ($K \in {8, 16}$) to compute the top-1/top-2 log-probability margin $\Delta{\log p}$ and truncated top-64 spectral Shannon entropy $\mathcal{H}_{64}$. Across both dense models (4B–8B) and mixture-of-experts architectures (gpt-oss-120b), LRS-RTE distinguishes memorized pattern matching from invariant algebraic deduction without requiring expensive full-length Chain-of-Thought rollouts.

1. Mechanistic Hypothesis: The Invariant Algebraic Manifold

Let $\mathcal{M}\theta$ be an autoregressive transformer with $L$ layers and residual stream dimension $d$. Given a mathematical problem statement $P$, the model maps the tokenized prompt to a sequence of hidden states $\mathbf{h}^{(l)}(P) \in \mathbb{R}^{|P| \times d}$ for $l \in {1, \dots, L}$. The final prompt token position $t = |P|$ acts as the Reasoning Launchpad State $\mathbf{z}^{(l)}(P) := \mathbf{h}{|P|}^{(l)}(P)$, summarizing the problem's formal structure immediately prior to emitting <think> tokens.

Consider an isomorphic operator $\mathcal{T}$ that renames bound algebraic variables ($x \leftrightarrow y, n \leftrightarrow k, a \leftrightarrow b$) and prepends a deductively neutral instruction. Because $\mathcal{T}(P)$ preserves the underlying mathematical group/ring structure of $P$, a model that has synthesized an invariant structural representation of the problem in its mid-to-late transformer blocks must satisfy:

$$\mathcal{S}{\cos}(P; \mathcal{M}\theta) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|} \sum_{l \in \mathcal{L}} \frac{\langle \mathbf{z}^{(l)}(P), \mathbf{z}^{(l)}(\mathcal{T}(P)) \rangle}{|\mathbf{z}^{(l)}(P)|_2 \, |\mathbf{z}^{(l)}(\mathcal{T}(P))|_2} \approx 1, \quad \mathcal{L} = \left{ \lfloor 0.5L \rfloor, \lfloor 0.75L \rfloor, \lfloor 0.9L \rfloor, L \right}$$

Conversely, when $\mathcal{M}\theta$ relies on surface-level n-gram contamination or heuristic template matching, renaming variables disrupts early-to-mid layer induction heads, causing rapid directional divergence in the residual stream ($\mathcal{S}{\cos} \ll 1$) and radial norm drift:

$$\mathcal{S}{\text{norm}}(P; \mathcal{M}\theta) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|} \sum_{l \in \mathcal{L}} \min\left( \frac{|\mathbf{z}^{(l)}(P)|_2}{|\mathbf{z}^{(l)}(\mathcal{T}(P))|_2}, \frac{|\mathbf{z}^{(l)}(\mathcal{T}(P))|_2}{|\mathbf{z}^{(l)}(P)|_2} \right)$$

2. Stage B: Early-Trace Spectral Entropy and Logit Margin

While $\mathcal{S}{\cos}$ and $\mathcal{S}{\text{norm}}$ capture pre-generation representational invariance, a model may still fail if its autoregressive transition dynamics enter a high-entropy "guessing regime" during the initial problem decomposition steps. Full-length reasoning rollouts ($2,048$–$8,192$ tokens) are computationally prohibitive within the 1-hour Codabench budget across 50 problems and 5 models. However, we observe that bifurcation instability manifests within the first $K \in [8, 16]$ reasoning tokens.

Let $\boldsymbol{\ell}k \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|}$ denote the vocabulary logits at reasoning step $k \in {1, \dots, K}$, and let $p_k = \text{softmax}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_k)$. We extract two white-box trajectory statistics: 1. Normalized Top-1 / Top-2 Log-Probability Margin ($\bar{\Delta}_{\log p}$): $$\bar{\Delta}{\log p} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \min\left( \frac{\log p_{k,(1)} - \log p_{k,(2)}}{6.0}, \, 1.0 \right)$$ where $p_{k,(1)} \ge p_{k,(2)}$ are the two largest token probabilities at step $k$. 2. Truncated Top-64 Spectral Confidence ($1 - \bar{\mathcal{H}}_{64}$): $$\bar{\mathcal{H}}{64} = \frac{1}{K \log 64} \sum{k=1}^{K} \left( - \sum_{j=1}^{64} \tilde{p}{k,(j)} \log \tilde{p}{k,(j)} \right), \quad \tilde{p}{k,(j)} = \frac{p{k,(j)}}{\sum_{m=1}^{64} p_{k,(m)}}$$

3. Structural Fragility Priors and Quantile-Stabilized Calibration

To account for intrinsic problem fragility—such as extremal combinatorics ("minimum/maximum number of ways"), modular arithmetic traps ($\ge 3$-digit constants), and deeply nested LaTeX operators—we combine the four white-box activations with a structural prior $\Phi(P) \in [0.05, 0.95]$ and reasoning_effort shift $\delta_{\text{effort}} \in {-0.06, 0.0, +0.06}$:

$$R(P; \mathcal{M}\theta, \text{effort}) = 0.38\,\mathcal{S}{\cos} + 0.12\,\mathcal{S}{\text{norm}} + 0.22\,\bar{\Delta}{\log p} + 0.14\,(1 - \bar{\mathcal{H}}{64}) + 0.14\,\Phi(P) + \delta{\text{effort}}$$

Finally, because raw hidden-state cosine geometry varies systematically across model families (Qwen3.5-4B vs. Olmo-3-7B-Think vs. gpt-oss-120b), applying a naive static threshold across all models induces calibration bias. Since are_robust receives the full batch of problems $\mathcal{P} = {P_1, \dots, P_N}$ for a given (model_id, reasoning_effort) pair, we compute a quantile-stabilized decision boundary:

$$\tau(\mathcal{M}\theta) = 0.65 \cdot \text{median}{i=1..N}\big(R(P_i; \mathcal{M}_\theta, \text{effort})\big) + 0.35 \cdot \tau_0, \quad \tau_0 = 0.52$$

and predict True (robust) if and only if $R(P_i; \mathcal{M}\theta, \text{effort}) \ge \tau(\mathcal{M}\theta)$.
